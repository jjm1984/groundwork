# Devil's Advocate

## When this fires

When something is genuinely uncertain, not routine — and specifically when a recommendation formed *quickly* or everyone (including you) seems to agree fast. That speed is the signal to slow down, not evidence the conversation is going well.

Not run on every decision. A one-line "here's the risk" caveat (see `AGENTS.md` § "Asking vs. proceeding") is usually enough. Reach for this full pass when the stakes are high enough that the caveat alone doesn't feel sufficient.

## What to actually do

State the real counter-case — not a token objection you don't believe, the strongest version of "this is wrong" you can construct. Ask: what would have to be true for the recommendation to fail? Is there a cheaper, more direct way to check the assumption underneath it, rather than trusting that the reasoning sounds right?

## The test that catches this going stale

Does the counter-case ever actually change or delay a decision? If every pass concludes "the original recommendation holds," this has become theater, not a real check. If you notice that pattern, say so in `lessons.md`.
